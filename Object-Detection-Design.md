# COGS 300 robot car — object detection and approach

Firmware and controller · November 21, 2026

## Set up the car

1. **Wiring.** Keep the front HC-SR04 on A0 (trigger) and A1 (echo). Connect the positional SG90 signal to **D1**, its power to a separate regulated servo supply rated for the installed SG90, and its ground to that supply and Arduino ground. Keep regulator positive outputs separate. D1 is shared with the optional standalone button. Use the normal UNO R4 WiFi build with the button disconnected. Resolve the previously reported board heating and resets before energizing this assembly.
2. **Align the bracket.** Mount the sensor level at the height of the distinguishing target feature. Measure from the axle to the forward-facing transducer face for the firmware body geometry. Mount the pivot laterally centered. The firmware assumes the face is directly above the pivot by default. For a forward offset, set `RADAR_FACE_AHEAD_OF_PIVOT_MM` to the measured millimetres and rebuild.
3. **Check angle and range with wheels stopped.** Open the USB controller using `Launch Robot Controller.command`, or take control on the car's Wi-Fi page. Start the stationary radar scan. Place one hard reflector straight ahead, then to the left and right. Check the bracket against physical −60°, 0° and +60° marks. On USB, the servo settings accept pulses for **right, centre, left** in that order. Reverse the endpoint order if the scan is mirrored. Keep each endpoint within measured mechanical travel. Settings take effect in RAM. Reapply them after a restart or set calibrated firmware defaults.
4. **Teach the target width.** Bench-scan the actual target and each distractor separately at the distances and orientations expected in the arena. The phone Wi-Fi page and USB controller offer 1°, 2°, 3° and 5° bench spacing. Compare the two sweep directions at each spacing to see whether the measured edges repeat. Use complete contours with both edges visible. Record the displayed **apparent width**, which includes the beam's response at that distance. Enter the narrowest interval that covers the target examples and excludes the distractors. Similar widths across the tested views leave identity unresolved. There is no target profile at boot.
5. **Set motion geometry.** Use the car's measured wheel calibration and tape polarity. Supply an actually clear starting radius around the axle if the course provides one. Measure around the axle position after the entire car clears the stripe. Include the tape boundary and any nearby obstacle when measuring. Zero means unknown. The program also checks the sonar observations before authorizing travel.
6. **Run.** Use **Scan** for stationary calibration. Start the wall-maze course on bare floor. Crossing the exit stripe activates object finding automatically. The uneven circular perimeter is sensed locally by the floor sensors. The three floor sensors must all see the strip during the same crossing. Entry requires a positive forward range and room to continue until the rear pivot envelope clears the strip. Once in the arena, perimeter tape interrupts pursuit. The car retraces encoder waypoints onto clear floor, checks pivot clearance, turns back toward its approach path and scans for the target again. USB control uses a heartbeat. On Wi-Fi the car continues its autonomous run when the phone disconnects.


## Aim

Drive from the wall maze to the designated arena object as quickly as the car can reliably identify it and verify a clear route. Use one scanning HC-SR04 on a positional SG90. The existing fixed right sonar can continue serving wall following. The car makes its decisions onboard. The controller webapp shows what it measured and why it moved.

## Approaches considered

| Approach | Where it helps | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Full sweep before each move | Simple reference behavior for timing and repeatability tests | Repeatedly measures sectors that cannot affect the next move |
| Keep the sensor aimed at the target | Quickly updates distance to an isolated object straight ahead | Can miss an obstruction beside the target or along an off-axis driving path |
| Adaptive scans, selected for the car | Wide discovery, narrower scans during pursuit, a different viewpoint when visibility is poor | Requires tracking the target between observations and checking the planned motion separately |

## One run

1. **Cross the exit marker.** During wall following, park the servo at calibrated forward. Recognize the entrance crossing only in the maze phase where that marker is expected. Creep across until all floor sensors clear the strip, with an encoder travel limit and timeout. Stop on incomplete crossing. Establish the expected marker pattern and allowable travel from the actual course. After entry, any tape contact immediately interrupts arena motion.
2. **Discover candidates.** Stop the wheels and sweep the calibrated front arc. Group adjacent valid echoes using their range difference and spatial separation. Retain the samples, angular span, apparent width and range of each group. If no candidate qualifies, pivot through uncovered sectors with overlap, checking space for the chassis corners before turning. Track which sectors have already been scanned at this position.
3. **Confirm identity.** Rescan likely candidates at finer spacing. Compare the contour with measured target and distractor examples at similar distances and orientations. Require repeatable agreement and separation from competing candidates. Leave the choice unresolved when the evidence overlaps. The lab's target property is still needed to define this comparison.
4. **Approach.** Store the chosen candidate's position relative to the car and update its predicted bearing and range using wheel odometry. Alternate short scans of its edges with scans across the intended driving corridor, accounting for chassis width, turn sweep, and clearance margin. Advance only through recently observed clearance, at a speed that leaves room for braking, sensing latency, and pose uncertainty. Treat missing returns as unknown. Stop when clearance or target evidence expires. Use the existing 20 cm bumper standoff provisionally until the lab finish criterion is known.
5. **Handle ambiguity.** If a new, closer echo appears where the target should be, stop and mark the path obstructed. Preserve the prior target estimate for a bounded reacquisition attempt. If an object hides the area of interest, choose an observed open side, pivot, drive a short checked segment, then scan from the displaced position. The car obtains this lateral change of viewpoint using its differential drive. If both sides lack clearance or a bounded search exhausts viable views, stop with a reason visible in the app.

## Scan acquisition and display

**First-turn clearance.** Measure the clear footprint just inside the entry strip against the chassis's full pivot radius. Use that clearance only if the course layout guarantees it for every run. Elsewhere, authorize turns and detours from recently observed free space, including space the car already traversed within odometry tolerance. A sector of missing echoes alone cannot authorize a detour. Include an open arena with no background echoes in testing to expose cases where this rule prevents progress.

Extend the staggered acquisition in `Robot/Sensors.cpp`. At each trigger capture the settled, calibrated commanded bearing, sonar pivot and transducer offsets, chassis pose, timestamp and sweep ID. An ordinary SG90 exposes no measured shaft angle. Keep fresh echo duration and validity through the processing pipeline so the integer-centimetre display field does not limit analysis. Schedule pings around the other sonar. Keep off-axis echoes out of every consumer that assumes a forward-facing sensor.

Reuse the tower's polar projection and same-bearing filtering. Show the latest sweep as connected runs of measured returns, breaking the trace at missing echoes and large depth jumps. Overlay the selected contour, range, apparent width, and predicted bearing while reacquiring. Mark older scans with their age and pose or clear them after movement. The outline represents the visible reflecting surface. Occluded regions stay blank. Keep the current sweep and candidate summaries in bounded onboard buffers, and send scan data to the controller without blocking motor updates.

## Calibration and verification order

1. Resolve the previously reported board heating and resets before powered car tests. Verify servo supply capacity under load, common ground, signal pin availability, motor PWM compatibility, mechanical stops and cable slack. Calibrate straight ahead with the actual bracket installed.
2. Use a pointer and angle scale to measure repeatability and settling time from both directions. Sweep the real objects at measured ranges with 5°, 2° and 1° spacing. Record apparent-width error, edge variability, smallest separable gap, missed echoes and scan duration. Pick discovery and confirmation spacing from the measurements. The nominal HC-SR04 measuring angle is 15° and its suggested measurement cycle exceeds 60 ms. [HC-SR04 datasheet](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/datasheets/Sensors/Proximity/HCSR04.pdf) · [SG90 specifications](https://towerpro.com.tw/product/sg90-analog/)
3. Replay recorded scans in the car controller and decision logic before floor runs. Include two objects at similar range, a nearer distractor, a hidden or absent target, and a stale scan after a pivot. Check that gaps stay visible and replays produce the expected lock, hold, or reacquisition decision.
4. Run the adaptive and full-sweep strategies from matching start poses on clear, cluttered and occluded layouts. Include a target initially behind the car, a target beside tape, and a gap narrower than the chassis. Record correct arrivals, wrong approaches, contacts, boundary crossings and completion time. Report failures alongside successful-run times. Choose the fastest settings that meet the measured identification and clearance requirements.

The deployed profile must come from the actual objects. Wheel calibration, marker geometry, the mounted servo angles and the provisional 200 mm finish distance still need confirmation on the course.
